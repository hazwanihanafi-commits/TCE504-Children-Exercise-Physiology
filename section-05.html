CLEAR SCIENCE FACTS · SECTION 5

Cardiac output during exercise

What the science says

Cardiac output is heart rate multiplied by stroke volume (Q̇ = HR × SV). Both variables contribute to meeting oxygen-delivery demands.

Apply it

Do not equate a higher heart rate with greater cardiac output without considering stroke volume.

Section screen 5: Running Economy

The child’s lower VO2max limits endurance performance.

Activities in which body weight is the major resistance to movement,

Children cannot maintain a running pace = differences in the economy of effort.

At a given speed on a treadmill, a child will have a substantially higher submaximal oxygen consumption when expressed relative to body weight than an adult.

As children age, their legs lengthen, their muscles become stronger, and their running skills improve.

Children rely more heavily on fat oxidation for fuel during exercise.

Section screen 10: Thermoregulation

Section screen 11: The temperature gradient between the body and its surrounding environment.

When exercising under unfavorable environmental conditions of high humidity and heat—the larger skin surface–to–body mass ratio of the child may actually increase the risk of hyperthermia.

This increased risk occurs because heat may be gained from, rather than lost to, the surrounding environment.
