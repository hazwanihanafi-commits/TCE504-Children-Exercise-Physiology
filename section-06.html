Section screen 5: High-intensity strength, power, speed and hypertrophy training

Physiological adaptations to high-intensity short-duration training types

Fibre size

Muscle size increases with hypertrophy training and to a lesser degree strength training- increase in the number and thickness of actin and myosin filaments, myofibrils and sarcoplasm (fluid within the muscle cell).

Fast-twitch fibre behaviour

Strength, hypertrophy, power and speed training modalities all focus on contractions from fast-twitch fibres

Nerve–muscle connections

Increased recruitment of additional motor units

Increased activation of synergistic muscles to assist force production

Neural pathways linking to target muscles become more efficient at transmitting the message (stimulus).

Section screen 7: Physiological adaptation to longer duration training types

Capillarisation

The number of capillaries in the muscle increase.

More O2 to be delivered to working muscles to be absorbed back into the blood

Substrate storage

Muscles and liver increase their ability to store carbohydrate and fat

Slow-twitch fibre behaviour

As the duration of training lengthens slow-twitch (endurance) fibres become increasingly dominant.

Mitochondria

The number of mitochondria increases with aerobic exercise enabling more aerobic metabolism to take place.

Section screen 8: Increased blood flow to working muscles

Increase in temperature

Blood also carries heat so the working muscles become

Increase in pliability

When the temperature of our muscles increases so does our

ability to stretch them.

Increased nerve to muscle connection

Leads to greater power output from our muscles.
